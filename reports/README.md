# Montra Hospitality Intel — Guest Experience Analytics & Sentiment Intelligence

Turning 35,000 guest reviews into evidence of **what** guests talk about, **how** they feel about it, and **where** Montra's hotels should act. The project combines BERTopic topic modelling, topic-level sentiment, a fine-tuned BERT-family classifier benchmarked against classical models, and experiment tracking with MLflow.

**Author:** Marc Aurel Ntjam Minkeng

## Results at a glance

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What drives satisfaction? | What guests say about specific aspects explains **74%** of rating variation (R² 0.742). Guest profile, room type, channel, price and stay length explain almost none. |
| Where are the problems? | At **property level**. Net sentiment spans 41.5 points across hotels but under 4 points across guest segments. The six weakest hotels are all 3★, each with its own signature complaint (value at Edinburgh Riverside #14 and Glasgow Harbour, staff at Edinburgh Riverside #9, food at Glasgow Skyline, cleanliness and room condition at The Glasgow Heritage, location at The Oxford Boutique). |
| What do guests complain about most? | **Pricing & Value** (2,029 negative mentions; highest rating impact per mention): extras charged on top of the rate and quality not matching the price. |
| Topics | BERTopic on aspect-focused RoBERTa-base embeddings: 29 topics (pricing, arrival, location, breakfast, spa, Wi-Fi, concierge, bathroom…) rolled up into 7 business aspects. Six configurations tracked in MLflow. |
| Sentiment model | Random Forest selected (test macro-F1 **0.942**, accuracy 0.941). Fine-tuned RoBERTa-base reaches 0.943, statistically tied. 52 sentiment runs tracked in MLflow. |
| Error analysis | 5.9% test error; **89% of errors** come from the ~5% of reviews whose label contradicts their rating. Where label and rating agree, accuracy is 99.3%. |

Full write-ups: [`reports/01_EDA_report.md`](reports/01_EDA_report.md), [`reports/02_model_evaluation_report.md`](reports/02_model_evaluation_report.md), [`reports/03_business_insight_report.md`](reports/03_business_insight_report.md).

## How to run

1. **Environment.** In the project's virtual environment: `pip install -r requirements.txt`.
2. **Database credentials.** Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and set `DATABASE_URL`. The `.env` file is git-ignored. Never put the connection string in a notebook. Without a `.env`, the notebook uses the snapshots in `data/raw/`.
3. **Run** `notebook/guest_experience_analysis.ipynb` top to bottom (about 12–15 minutes on a laptop).
   - Topic embeddings are cached in `data/processed/sentence_embeddings.npz` and the transformer results in `data/processed/bert_results.json`. A re-run therefore reproduces the reference results without retraining.
   - To retrain the transformer, set `RUN_BERT = True` in the setup cell. That takes about 1–1.5 hours on a CPU-only laptop, or a few minutes on a GPU such as Google Colab.
   - To recompute the topic embeddings, delete `sentence_embeddings.npz`. With Hugging Face access the notebook then uses `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, the model the brief recommends; topic numbers will change slightly and the topic insights in Phase 5 should be re-read against the new output.
4. **Experiments.** From the project root: `mlflow ui --backend-store-uri sqlite:///mlruns/mlflow.db`, then open http://127.0.0.1:5000.

## Notes on the reference run

The reference execution ran without access to huggingface.co. The transformer weights (RoBERTa-base) were therefore loaded from a local copy: spaCy's `en_core_web_trf` release on GitHub, converted to Hugging Face format. Same architecture and vocabulary, but spaCy had additionally trained it on English tagging, parsing and entity recognition. With internet access, the notebook downloads the original `roberta-base` instead, and uses `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` for topic embeddings when the embedding cache is deleted.

The pipeline is deterministic: two executions from the same caches produce identical outputs.
